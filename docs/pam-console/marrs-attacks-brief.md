# Marrs Attacks: Split-Screen Explainer

**Strategic brief**
**For:** the Marrs Attacks marketing manager agent
**Companion document:** *Marrs Split-Screen Explainer, Generation Rules*, which covers how to build one. This brief covers what it is and why it exists.

---

## 1. The brand

Marrs Attacks is built on one idea: interesting questions, answered in interesting ways.

The identity is a builder who sees the system behind the mess. Someone who asks the questions people carry around silently and answers them like he is explaining it to a friend. No guru energy.

The throughline is amplifying how people do their work so they can be more potent and achieve what they are actually here to do. **AI is the multiplier, not the point.** The point is what someone becomes capable of when they have the right tools and the right frame.

## 2. The audience

Working professionals. Mostly white collar, some blue collar. People who are curious about AI and unsure, or just starting.

This is deliberately not the early adopter crowd. Globally, far more people are outside the AI conversation than inside it, and that is the audience. The editorial job is **translation**: taking enterprise-grade AI reality and making it useful for someone's Monday.

Marrs is credible here for a specific reason. He builds with AI daily and understands the security and compliance side of enterprise deployment. That combination is rare, and it is what separates commentary from experience.

## 3. What the format is

A split screen. Top frame is Marrs, torso up. Bottom frame is a bird's-eye view of the touchscreen on his desk. A strip of desk sits between them where his hands cross.

He walks on holding a countdown timer and says "In under 60 seconds I'm going to explain to you why...". The timer travels down and crosses the frame split, which pulls the viewer's eye into the bottom frame. It lands on the desk and he finishes the sentence with the title, which is already on the screen. He hits Go and delivers a four-beat argument, tapping the screen once per beat to advance an animated sequence. The timer stops on camera with time still on it.

Three hooks fire at once: the visual walk-on, the spoken promise, and the written title.

## 4. Why we are doubling down

It already works. In current reporting the split-screen explainer is running at 5 to 10 times everything else on the account. Nothing else in the format mix is close.

The strategic reason is stronger than the number. The format is a machine for the brand's core promise. An interesting question, answered in an interesting way, in under a minute, with proof that the promise was kept. The timer is not decoration. It is a public commitment made and then visibly honoured, which is how the account earns trust without claiming authority.

The format also compounds. Every piece looks like every other piece, so the account becomes recognisable in a feed, and platforms learn what the account is about.

## 5. What makes one work

**The question is the product.** Everything else is execution.

A question earns a place only if it does one of two things:

- **Contradicts something the viewer believes**, leaving a gap they cannot close on their own
- **Promises an outcome they want** and do not have

The killer test: if the viewer can answer the title themselves, there is nothing to click. Accurate descriptions of the viewer's behaviour feel true and generate no curiosity. This is the most common failure mode.

The question set was validated across seven rounds of scoring, ending at 90 percent. The full rules, including vocabulary sourcing and the failure patterns, live in the companion document.

## 6. The four pillars

| Pillar | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Translation** | What this AI news or shift actually means for your work |
| **Myth-busting** | The confident wrong thing everyone repeats |
| **Future** | Where this goes, reasoned rather than prophesied |
| **Proof of work** | What Marrs built, and what broke |

Pillar 4 is the one nobody can copy, and it is already the best evidenced. The account's single best performing piece to date was an honest struggle story at 44,600 views and 552 saves. That is pillar 4 in a different format, and it is worth testing whether split-screen can carry it.

**News is a trigger, not a subject.** When an event lands, the question is what an ordinary person fears or hopes about it. The event itself appears in beat 1 only, never in the title. This keeps titles alive for months and lets a piece that underperforms be re-pegged rather than rewritten.

## 7. Where it sits in the mix

Split-screen is the hero format. One narrative for Marrs produces 8 to 10 pieces across LinkedIn first, then YouTube, TikTok and Instagram. The split-screen anchors the set and other formats hang off it.

It is not the only format and should not become one. Talking-to-camera shorts, long-form YouTube and LinkedIn text all carry load. Podcast clips run daily and automatically as baseline.

## 8. Production reality

This constrains planning more than anything else in this brief.

- A split-screen costs roughly 45 to 90 minutes to set up, record and edit. Treat that figure as an estimate until real numbers replace it
- They get batched. Three to four back to back in one session, one recording day a month
- A talking-to-camera short costs 15 to 30 minutes

**Consequence:** split-screens carry trends and durable questions, which tolerate a monthly batch. Anything with a 48 hour window goes to talking-to-camera instead. Planning a split-screen against breaking news will fail on the calendar, not on the idea.

## 9. What success looks like

Marrs Attacks is a **growth** engine. It builds audience and authority. It does not run a funnel, and it does not carry Polynize use cases.

The CTA is a follow, spoken after the timer stops, outside the clock.

Measure in this order:

1. **Saves and shares.** A save carries roughly five times the weight of a like, and a share means the piece was worth sending to a specific person. Relief-shaped questions get sent, shame-shaped questions only get watched
2. **Views**, as the reach signal
3. **Follows**, as the compounding signal

Current baseline for Marrs is about 8,500 monthly views on LinkedIn, with 3,648 followers there, 387 on TikTok, 900 on Instagram and 97 on YouTube. Growth targets beyond that are proposals, not commitments.

## 10. What this format is not for

- **Polynize use cases.** Different job, different rules. Polynize sells a problem, Marrs Attacks offers relief. The question rules invert and must not be reused
- **Breaking news** inside 48 hours. Wrong production shape
- **Ideas that need more than 140 words.** If it cannot survive the budget, it is not this format

## 11. What is not yet proven

Stated plainly so nothing here is mistaken for settled:

- The question rules are validated. The script structure and visual grammar come from one worked example and have not been tested in production
- The 5 to 10 times figure is a current snapshot, not a permanent property
- Production time estimates need replacing with real numbers, and the split-screen figure matters most because it sets the whole cadence
